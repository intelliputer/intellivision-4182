# Proposed 16-bit TTL video controller

## Purpose

This document defines a buildable first version of the MAGIC-inspired video controller. It is a **16-bit-wide video-memory and display-fetch subsystem** controlled by a 6309 over an 8-bit register interface. It is not a 16-bit CPU bus and does not attempt to reproduce the undocumented MAGIC ASIC.

No FPGA, CPLD, GAL, or other programmable logic is permitted. The controller uses the available 5 V **74LS TTL** family throughout. SRAM, ROM, a crystal oscillator, and analogue video parts are exceptions because they are memories, clock sources, or analogue components rather than programmable logic.

## First target

| Item | Initial value | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CPU | HD63C09/6309 | Available processor; 8-bit external data bus and 16-bit addresses/registers. |
| Video output | One RCA jack carrying baseband composite through the sibling `video-composites` board | An external RCA-to-HDMI converter is responsible for display connection; the shared board buffers/amplifies a composite waveform and is not an RGB-to-composite encoder. |
| Audio output | One 3.5 mm TRS female jack | Tip = left, ring = right, sleeve = audio ground. Audio is integrated from the ECS or provided by an ECS additional-PSG re-creation; it is not generated by the video controller. |
| Logical framebuffer | 320 × 240 pixels, 4 bits per pixel | A 240p-style non-interlaced NTSC first target; 16 colours are sufficient to prove the datapath. |
| VRAM organisation | 32K × 16 bits (64 KiB) | 320 × 240 × 4 bpp consumes 19,200 words, leaving room for tables/double buffering experiments. |
| Video word | Four consecutive 4-bit pixels | A 16-bit word maps naturally to the paired SRAM banks. |
| First timing target | 14.31818 MHz NTSC master clock; 7.15909 MHz pixel/controller clock; 3.579545 MHz colour subcarrier | Keeps the complex 74LS datapath at a conservative clock rate while deriving timing coherent with NTSC colour. |
| Bus policy | Display has priority; CPU commands use defined free slots | Avoids any simultaneous drive of video RAM. |

The controller has one output responsibility: generate a stable, valid baseband composite waveform for the RCA output chain. An external RCA-to-HDMI converter handles HDMI/display compatibility. The converter does not generate missing sync or colour encoding, so the controller and composite board still need one internally consistent source profile; multi-standard television support is out of scope.

## Memory and display datapath

### VRAM

VRAM is formed by two equal 32K × 8 asynchronous SRAMs. Their address, chip-enable, and output-enable signals are shared. One receives/drives D0–D7; the other receives/drives D8–D15. Together they behave as a 32K × 16 word store.

The display engine fetches one word for every four logical pixels. It latches that word, then uses a two-bit pixel-within-word counter to select one of its four nibbles. The selected nibble addresses a 16-entry palette. Each palette entry produces digital red, green, and blue components for the controller's composite-encoder stage.

The initial visible framebuffer begins at word address zero:

```text
320 pixels ÷ 4 pixels/word = 80 words per logical scan line
80 words × 240 logical scan lines = 19,200 words
```

The first target presents one framebuffer line per visible scan line. The timing generator provides borders and blanking around the 320-pixel active portion of each line.

## Composite-board integration

The sibling [video-composites project](../../video-composites/README.md) defines boards that buffer/amplify **baseband composite video**, typically from a console signal ahead of its RF modulator. Its Intellivision analysis explicitly states that the board does not generate colour information; it AC-couples and amplifies the existing composite waveform.

Accordingly, the video controller's integration point is the composite board's `VIDEO_IN`/`VID_IN`, not its RCA output. The controller must supply a verified, pre-amplifier composite waveform containing sync, blanking, black level, colour burst, luminance, and chrominance. The composite board then provides the 75-ohm-capable output stage and connector arrangement.

Before connecting the projects, define and measure this contract on the actual board revision:

| Item | Required decision/measurement |
| --- | --- |
| Source profile | Choose one internally consistent composite timing/colour profile that the selected RCA-to-HDMI converter accepts. Multi-standard support is out of scope. |
| Input DC bias | Match the coupling/bias assumptions of the chosen composite-board input. |
| Composite amplitude | Verify at the composite board's input and at a 75-ohm terminated output; do not assume a TTL-level signal is acceptable. |
| Source impedance and termination | Confirm whether the controller needs a series resistor, an emitter-follower/buffer, or an attenuator before `VIDEO_IN`. |
| Sync polarity and level | Scope the waveform through the amplifier while checking stable lock, black level, and bright-scene clipping. |
| Grounding | Use one short, controlled video return and keep digital-clock return currents away from the analogue input path. |

The composite encoder is therefore a controller subassembly to be designed and validated with the video-composites project. It is not replaced by the existing two-transistor amplifier board.

### ECS audio integration / additional-PSG re-creation

The controller has no audio DAC, PSG, or mixer in this revision. Audio is routed separately to one **3.5 mm stereo female (TRS) jack**: tip is left, ring is right, and sleeve is the audio return. The ECS itself provides three additional PSG tone channels and noise through its AY-3-8917; mixed with the Master Component's AY-3-8914, the original system produces a mono six-note output.

Two audio paths are in scope:

| Path | Description | Initial use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Integrate original ECS audio** | Take the existing post-mix, pre-RF/baseband mono audio from an original Master Component + ECS. AC-couple and buffer it to the TRS jack. | Fastest route to authentic ECS sound while the original hardware is present. |
| **Use the ECS PSG** | The 6309 sends sound commands through the CP1610 mailbox; CP1610-side code writes the existing ECS AY-3-8917 registers. The physical PSG remains ECS-owned. | Preferred way to make the companion use the real ECS-added sound chip without modifying ECS traces. |
| **Direct ECS PSG access modification** | Cut/splice selected ECS AY-3-8917 control/data tracks and insert buffered, TTL-arbitrated access from the daughterboard. At any instant, either the ECS or 6309 interface owns the PSG pins. | Experimental option only, after the mailbox path and complete signal measurements work. |
| **ECS additional-PSG re-creation** | Add a real AY-3-8917, its clock/reset, decoupling, analogue output/mixer, and a 6309/TTL register interface. This re-creates the ECS's added three-tone/noise subsystem without copying the PSG silicon in TTL. | Standalone 6309/MAGIC companion operation and eventual replacement experiments. |

Both paths are mono at first. Present mono at the stereo jack by buffering/splitting it to tip and ring through separate series resistors or an active dual buffer; never directly join two driven stereo outputs. Stereo is a later enhancement requiring intentionally separate left/right source and mixing paths.

The additional-PSG re-creation must not be called ECS-compatible until its PSG clock, register decode, amplitude/mixing network, and controller-port behaviour are measured against original hardware. A full six-channel standalone reconstruction additionally requires the Master Component's AY-3-8914 path or a compatible replacement; the added AY-3-8917 provides only the ECS-added three channels.

Direct physical access to the existing ECS PSG is permitted as an experimental branch. Any track cut or drilled splice must be documented with before/after photographs and continuity measurements, use removable jumpers or zero-ohm links where possible, and isolate every injected signal through a buffer/transceiver. Never allow the ECS interface and the daughterboard to drive an AY-3-8917 address, data, or control pin simultaneously.

Keep the audio return and the composite-video return controlled at the output area to avoid clock-noise coupling.

### Address generation

The controller needs three independently latched address values:

- `LINE_BASE`: the first VRAM word of the current logical scan line.
- `FETCH_ADDR`: the word currently being fetched; it increments once per four logical pixels.
- `CPU_ADDR`: the address held by a queued CPU command.

At the end of a logical line, TTL adders calculate `LINE_BASE + 80`; the result becomes the next line's base address. At the start of each displayed line pair, `FETCH_ADDR` is loaded from `LINE_BASE`. This is preferable to attempting a real-time multiply of X and Y coordinates.

## Timing and arbitration

Three 4-bit synchronous counters create a horizontal count of 0–454 at 7.15909 MHz, yielding the NTSC line rate. Another three create the vertical count; the exact 262/263-line frame treatment must be chosen and scoped during the non-interlaced timing implementation. Equality comparators or decoded counter outputs generate visible-area, sync, blanking, and burst-gate signals. The 14.31818 MHz master clock may drive only simple divide-by-two/divide-by-four distribution; the RAM, address, palette, and arbitration datapaths run at or below 7.15909 MHz. Keep clock traces short and decouple every IC locally.

During active display, the fetch sequencer takes one RAM cycle at the beginning of each four-logical-pixel group. During blanking—and any non-fetch slot that timing analysis proves safe—the arbiter may service a queued CPU read or write. Only one sequencer may enable VRAM address/data/control signals at a time.

The first revision should not stall the 6309. Software writes a command, then polls `VSTATUS.READY`. A later revision may evaluate the 6309 `HALT` input after scope/logic-analyser validation of every bus cycle.

## 6309-visible register interface

The actual base address is selected by the 6309-board address decoder. All registers are 8 bits wide.

| Offset | Register | Access | Meaning |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | `VADDR_LO` | W | VRAM word-address bits 0–7. |
| 1 | `VADDR_HI` | W | VRAM word-address bits 8–14; upper bit reserved. |
| 2 | `VDATA_LO` | R/W | Low byte of the staged 16-bit word. |
| 3 | `VDATA_HI` | R/W | High byte of the staged 16-bit word. |
| 4 | `VCMD` | W | `READ_WORD`, `WRITE_WORD`, `INC_ADDR`, display enable, and later palette/sprite commands. |
| 5 | `VSTATUS` | R | `READY`, `READ_VALID`, blanking state, FIFO full, frame IRQ, and raster IRQ. |
| 6 | `VIRQ_ACK` | W | Clears the frame and/or raster interrupt latch. |
| 7 | `VRASTER` | W | Initial programmable raster-line compare value; wider value may use a second register. |

### Safe word-write sequence

1. Write `VADDR_LO` and `VADDR_HI`.
2. Write `VDATA_LO` and `VDATA_HI`.
3. Write `WRITE_WORD` to `VCMD`.
4. Poll `VSTATUS.READY` before reusing the staging registers.

The command latch only asserts VRAM write-enable when both bytes and a write command have been captured. The display engine therefore never observes a partially written 16-bit word.

### Streaming-word mode

For bulk uploads, `VCMD` must also provide a `STREAM_WRITE` mode: set `VADDR` once, enable auto-increment once, then write alternating `VDATA_LO` and `VDATA_HI` bytes. Each `VDATA_HI` write commits one complete word and increments `VADDR`. This reduces a sequential upload from three CPU I/O writes per word to two. `VSTATUS.READY` still guards against an overrun.

## Performance expectation

The video output itself refreshes independently at the selected composite frame rate. The CPU does **not** need to redraw unchanged pixels for the display to remain stable. What is limited is how much new framebuffer data the 6309 can upload each frame.

The 320 × 240, 4bpp framebuffer contains 19,200 16-bit words (38,400 bytes). In streaming mode, a complete rewrite needs 38,400 byte writes. The reference M6x09-II configuration (`E` ≈ 1.8432 MHz) is expected to manage about **4–7 full-screen updates per second** in real code. With the stocked HD63C09RP, a separately populated performance board may use at most a 12 MHz crystal (`E` = 3 MHz); after CPU, ACIA, power, expansion-bus, and TTL-interface acceptance, **roughly 8–12 full-screen updates per second** is the planning range. The 14.31818 MHz source is the video master clock and must not be fitted as the stock CPU crystal. Neither CPU configuration makes 60 Hz full-screen framebuffer animation the right design target.

This is not how the Master Component normally achieves animation. Its CP1610 runs at roughly 0.895 MHz, but the STIC draws its cards and moving objects in hardware. Small object/register updates can therefore appear at the television frame rate without repainting the screen. The first framebuffer-only controller will match the Master Component's *60 Hz display refresh*, but not its most efficient object-animation method when a full screen changes.

To match or exceed the Master Component's perceived drawing speed, add hardware features after the framebuffer path works:

- card/tile maps so backgrounds change by table entry rather than pixels;
- a sprite/moving-object engine fed by position/attribute registers;
- palette changes and raster effects for low-bandwidth animation;
- a command FIFO so the 6309 queues updates while the display fetcher owns VRAM.

With those features, the 6309's job is to update a small command/state set each frame—similar in spirit to the CP1610/STIC division—while the TTL controller performs the repetitive pixel work.

The assumptions, calculations, comparison criteria, and required measurements are tracked separately in [video performance analysis](VIDEO-PERFORMANCE-ANALYSIS.md).

## Interrupts

Two open-collector TTL interrupt requests are proposed:

- **Frame IRQ:** asserted at the start of vertical blanking. It is the normal time for bulk framebuffer updates.
- **Raster IRQ:** asserted when the vertical counter equals `VRASTER`. It is optional in the first board, but preserves a central MAGIC idea.

Both requests are latched until `VIRQ_ACK`. They must be level-translated/buffered as appropriate for the 6309 interrupt input selected by the CPU board.

## Bring-up order

1. Prove regulated 5 V power, decoupling, oscillator, and reset with no CPU or VRAM installed.
2. Build timing counters and prove stable blank/sync timing at test points.
3. Add a fixed test-pattern generator before adding VRAM; use it to validate the composite encoder and video-composites input/output chain.
4. Add one SRAM bank, then both banks, and verify non-contentious 16-bit read/write cycles with a logic analyser.
5. Add display fetch, word latch, nibble selection, and a fixed palette.
6. Add the 6309 register interface and prove the safe word-write/read protocol.
7. Add frame IRQ, raster IRQ, palette writes, and finally a command queue.
8. Only then investigate a mailbox bridge to the Master Component/ECS.

## Open questions

- Which exact 6309 package/speed grade is available, and is it guaranteed 5 V TTL compatible?
- Which composite-board revision and RCA-to-HDMI converter form the first integration contract, and which source profile does that converter demonstrably accept?
- What composite-encoder topology can produce the required baseband waveform using the permitted TTL and analogue parts?
- Which path is first: original ECS audio integration, mailbox-mediated use of the existing ECS PSG, direct ECS PSG access modification, or the additional-PSG re-creation?
- Where is the verified post-mix, pre-RF/baseband audio tap on the target Master Component/ECS revision, and what are its level and impedance?
- Should the first palette be fixed, register-programmable, or RAM-programmable?
- Is a full 320 × 240 × 4 bpp framebuffer necessary for revision one, or should a 40 × 24 card mode be brought up first?
- Which physical connector will be permitted for any future bridge to the Intellivision/ECS, and what are its measured electrical limits?
